## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your work is built on.** "Built on myPKA by Paperless Movement." "A myPKA setup for architects." "My ICOR-based workflow."
- **Use the names in ordinary speech:** articles, videos, courses, talks, reviews, comparisons, tutorials and criticism.
- **Keep the credit lines** the licenses already ask for.
- **Use myPKA at work and for clients,** and say so.

## What needs our written permission

- **Naming your product, agent pack, template set, course or service after ours**, for example "myPKA Pro", "myPKA Agents", "ICOR Plus", or anything a reasonable person could take for an official product.
- **Using our logos or names as your branding:** product name, logo, app icon, company name, domain name, social handle.
- **Implying endorsement, partnership or certification** that does not exist in writing. No "official", "certified", "authorized" or "partner" unless we said so in writing.
- **Merchandise** carrying the marks.

## The line in one sentence

Use the marks to say what your work is built on. Do not use them to say who your work is by.

## Your own version and your own agents

If you publish a changed myPKA or agents built on it, make clear it is yours: "Acme Vault, built on myPKA. Not affiliated with Paperless Movement, S.L." Not: "myPKA by Acme".

## Asking

Permission requests and edge cases: contact@myicor.com. A short email is cheaper than a rebrand.

## How we enforce

We act against confusion about who made something, not against conversation. Descriptive and critical uses are welcome, including ones we do not like.
